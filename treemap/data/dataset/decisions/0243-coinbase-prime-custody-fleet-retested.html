Explanation

Validators withdrawing to these 171 addresses are the stake of Coinbase Prime custody clients, run by Coinbase on its own validator fleet; Coinbase is the allocator, as decision 0134 finds for other custody addresses. In October 2026 the addresses held about 3,230 active validators with some 128k ETH.

Why these were not in decision 0134. Coinbase holds all of these addresses in custody (decision 0167). Decision 0134 tested custody addresses for Coinbase's fleet only among those not attributed at the time; these were then attributed by an earlier decision, 0114, later replaced by the record of custody alone, so they were left in "Coinbase custody clients, provider not identified" without the test.

The test. It is decision 0134's. Clients write their version into block graffiti as a short build string. Since February 2025 these addresses' validators proposed 473 blocks with a build string, and every one carries a build that Coinbase's own retail validators were running at the time; none carries a build only Galaxy's validators run, and none names an operator. 106 of the addresses have validators with such blocks. The rest mostly leave graffiti blank, as Coinbase's retail validators do, and are tied to the others by their fee recipients: resolving the builder's payment in each block, 150 of the addresses pay a recipient that receives at least 90% of its block payments from validators in Coinbase's custody and staking, and that addresses of decisions 0134, 0226 or 0242 also pay. Every one of the 116 client groups formed this way includes validators running Coinbase's builds or addresses already found to be on its fleet.

What was left out. Of the 675 custody addresses tested, 504 remain as they were: those whose validators never emit a build string and share no client fee recipient with Coinbase's fleet (about 456k ETH), those whose client group also runs another operator's builds (about 194k ETH), and those where other validators at the same address sign Galaxy's name, even when linked to Coinbase's fleet (about 95k ETH). For these, custody alone does not show who runs the validators.

Limits. Build strings appear in a small share of blocks, and for 65 addresses the evidence is the shared fee recipient alone, which shows the same client rather than the same operator; a client using Coinbase for some validators and another provider for others would look the same where the other provider leaves graffiti blank. Coinbase reports running validators holding about 4.5M ETH in the first quarter of 2026, excluding stake routed to partner providers. The decision would change with an operator's name in these validators' graffiti, a build mix diverging from Coinbase's fleet, or Coinbase saying that this stake is run by partners.

What would change it

A named operator in these validators' graffiti, a build mix diverging from Coinbase's fleet, or Coinbase saying this stake is run by partners.

Evidence

Transactions and contract calls anyone can repeat on their own node.
