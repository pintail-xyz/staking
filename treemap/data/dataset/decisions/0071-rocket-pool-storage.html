Explanation

The contract 0x1d8f…fa46 is RocketStorage, the registry in which Rocket Pool records the addresses of its contracts. The pools created by the contracts it registers as pool creators (minipools, and since the Saturn upgrade megapools) are Rocket Pool's, and validators withdrawing to them are attributed to Rocket Pool as allocator. 44,533 validators have withdrawn to 42,362 minipools and 113 megapools; Rocket Pool held 3.1% of all stake at its peak in 2023, and about 13,200 validators with 0.42M ETH in October 2026.

Identification. Rocket Pool's own node software gives this address as its mainnet storage contract. Reading the registry on chain, block by block, gives the pool-creating contract in force at each time: the minipool manager from November 2021, the minipool factories after the Redstone and Atlas upgrades, and the megapool factory after Saturn. Every Rocket Pool minipool and megapool was created by one of them. The node deposit contracts registered at each time made 41,603 first deposits, all withdrawing to pools of these creators. The pool-creator rule also finds 162 solo validators that joined Rocket Pool between April 2023 and June 2024 by changing their BLS credentials to an Atlas minipool; they are counted as Rocket Pool's only from the change.

Why Rocket Pool is the allocator. Node operators join without permission and bond part of each validator's stake; the protocol assigns rETH holders' ETH to them by its own rules. Treating the protocol rather than each node operator as the allocator is a framing choice, discussed among the method's failure modes. No operator is recorded.

Explorer labels on Rocket Pool's contracts are recorded as leads. The decision would change if permissionless node operators were treated as the allocators of their own validators, or if a pool creator were found not to be Rocket Pool's.

What would change it

Treating Rocket Pool's permissionless node operators as allocators of their own validators, or a pool creator found not to be Rocket Pool's.
