Explanation

BlackRock's iShares Staked Ethereum Trust (ETHB) is the principal of the validators withdrawing to these 59 addresses: the ETH is the trust's, held in custody by Coinbase. In November 2026 the addresses held 277 active validators with some 337,300 ETH.

The match with the trust's figures. The trust's daily holdings file gave 337,754 ETH staked on 2 November 2026, and its quarterly report 250,585 ETH on 30 June 2026. These addresses' validators held about 337,300 ETH on 4 November, and 249,943 ETH had been deposited to them by 30 June: both within 0.3%. Each address sent its own deposits through Coinbase's batch deposit contract to validators withdrawing to the same address. Seven seed deposits on 7 March 2026, before the trust listed, split their ether 53% / 47% between Galaxy and Figment; from 13 March, 61 deposits split it 40% / 33% / 27% between Galaxy, Figment and Attestant. One address made deposits of both kinds.

Who chose the operators. The trust's prospectus says that its sponsor chooses the staking providers and allocates ether among them, and names the approved validators as Figment, Galaxy and Attestant. Coinbase is therefore the custodian of this stake, not its allocator; each provider allocates its share (decisions 0329, 0330 and 0331). For the one validator outside those shares, a 32 ETH deposit made on 6 March and since withdrawn, the trust itself is taken as the allocator.

The decision would change if the trust's staked ether diverged from these addresses' validators, or with evidence that the addresses hold another client's ether.

What would change it

The trust's staked ether diverging from these addresses' validators, or evidence that the addresses hold another client's ether.
