Explanation

These 1,614 validators, at fifteen Coinbase Prime custody addresses whose other validators Figment runs, are attributed to Figment as allocator. For them this takes precedence over the custody decisions covering those addresses (0134 and 0167). In October 2026, 1,220 of them were active with about 47k ETH.

The link to Figment. Eleven of the fifteen addresses also hold validators deposited through Figment's documented batch contract, 973 in all, or validators decision 0137 identifies as Figment's. Coinbase Prime clients choose their staking provider, and these clients chose Figment for those validators.

Figment's fleet behaviour. Every recorded gas-limit vote from these validators, 232 of 232, was against raising the limit to 60 million, as Figment's own fleet voted, whereas Coinbase's fleet voted for it. None of their 2,640 blocks delivered through relays came through the Aestus or Agnostic relays, as with Figment's fleet; most of Coinbase's fleet used those relays for about a fifth of its blocks. None of their blocks carries one of the client build strings Coinbase's fleet signs.

Limits. The relay test alone would not separate them from Coinbase, since a few of Coinbase's retail addresses also never used those relays; but those sign Coinbase's build strings and split their gas votes. Four of the fifteen addresses have no validators deposited through Figment's contract and rest on behaviour alone. The decision would change with Coinbase or the custody clients naming another operator for these validators, or their behaviour diverging from Figment's fleet.

What would change it

Coinbase or the custody clients naming another operator for these validators, or their behaviour diverging from Figment's fleet.
