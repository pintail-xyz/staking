Explanation

The account 0x8beb…5aa4 makes deposits for Kraken's staking, restaked through an EigenLayer pod it owns; its validators are attributed to Kraken's staking as allocator. It deposited 594 validators between December 2025 and May 2026, all to its pod; in October 2026, after consolidation, 115 compounding validators were active with some 0.14M ETH.

Restaking. Every deposit goes to an EigenLayer pod created through EigenLayer's pod manager and owned by this account, so the withdrawals are the account's. Kraken announced in October 2024 that it would restake customers' staked ETH through EigenLayer, so pods owned by Kraken's accounts are expected. Restaking delegates the pod's stake to an EigenLayer operator for additional services; it does not change who runs the validators, and so does not affect the allocator. The account delegates to an operator whose own published metadata names it "Kraken [2]", though anyone can delegate to an operator.

The link to Kraken. The account's first gas came in November 2025 from Kraken's EigenLayer restaking account (decision 0010), within the same 40 minutes as the first gas of two other such accounts (decisions 0011 and 0013). Its principal, 5,000 ETH, came just before its first deposit from a wallet that funds Kraken's staking. It deposited through the same batch deposit contract as Kraken's main deposit account (decision 0007), and its pod consolidated its validators in June 2026, the same weeks as Kraken's other accounts.

The batch contract has no caller check, so using it does not by itself show that Kraken sent a deposit. Whether Kraken holds this stake for itself or for a client, Kraken chose who runs the validators. Explorer labels are recorded as leads. The decision would change with a Kraken statement contradicting it, or evidence that the account deposits keys a client chose.

What would change it

A Kraken statement contradicting it, or evidence it deposits keys chosen by a client.
