Explanation

Bitvavo, the Dutch exchange, is the principal of the validators withdrawing to these 39 addresses: its stake held at Coinbase Prime. Coinbase Prime remains their allocator (decision 0134) and Coinbase their custodian; this decision names the client. In October 2026 the addresses held 2,415 active validators with about 96k ETH.

Bitvavo's wallets. In a discussion on the ENS governance forum, Bitvavo identified 0x95b5… as its hot wallet to a customer, and a refund sent there reached the customer's account. The cold wallet 0xedc6…87c9 refills that hot wallet (decision 0215).

The fee recipient. Of the blocks these validators proposed, 99.7% paid the same fee recipient, 0x13f2…f334. Its outgoing payments went to the cold wallet 0xedc6…87c9 (500 ETH) and to 0x079a…dc63 (800 ETH), a second hot wallet, which pays the named hot wallet's gas and returns ETH to the cold wallet.

Direct payments. Twelve of the 39 addresses have sent ETH straight to these wallets, often several in one block: five addresses paid the cold wallet 8,576 ETH in a single block on 14 August 2025, and ten paid 0x079a…dc63 within a few days in May 2024. The other 27 are tied to Bitvavo only through the shared fee recipient.

Bitvavo and Coinbase Prime. Bitvavo's proof of reserves at 30 June 2026 states that some of its assets are held at Coinbase Prime. Coinbase runs these validators, by the client-build test of decision 0134.

Limits. Bitvavo has not named the fee recipient or the custody addresses. The decision would change with evidence that the fee recipient or these addresses serve another Coinbase Prime client, or that Bitvavo's transfers to them were payments to customers.

What would change it

Evidence that 0x13f2 or these addresses serve another Coinbase Prime client, or that Bitvavo's transfers to them were customer payments.
