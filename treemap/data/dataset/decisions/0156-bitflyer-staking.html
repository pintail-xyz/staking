AttributionAllocator bitFlyer staking · decided 2026-10-22

Validators withdrawing to this address are attributed to bitFlyer's staking (strongly suggestive, not conclusive).

Grounds

  • First partybitFlyer launched ETH staking on 12 August 2025; these validators' first large deposits were made that afternoon, Japan time
  • First partybitFlyer's audited accounts show 272,751 ETH held at end-2025; these validators then held 245,670 ETH
  • On chainAll deposits go through the 3-of-3 multisig they withdraw to, which got 256,246 ETH from one wallet

Explanation

Validators withdrawing to the multisig wallet 0x51b4…b913 are attributed to the ETH staking of bitFlyer, the Japanese exchange. The evidence is strongly suggestive but not conclusive: bitFlyer names no address, and the link rests on timing and size. 11,922 validators have been deposited since May 2025; about 8,080 were active in October 2026 with some 0.26M ETH. Most were deposited through Kiln's and Blockdaemon's contracts, so those providers are their allocators; this decision applies only to validators no provider claims, and decision 0188 records bitFlyer as principal of all of them.

The multisig. It is a multisig wallet with three owners, all of whom must sign. Every deposit was sent through it by its owners. One wallet sent it 256,246 ETH in 30 transfers from May 2025 to February 2026, and it is its validators' own fee recipient.

The timing. bitFlyer's staking page launched ETH staking for customers who opt in, with the amount staked to be increased in stages; its business report for 2025 confirms the launch in August 2025, and CoinPost dates it to 12 August. After two test deposits in May, this multisig began its large deposits at 17:21 Japan time on 12 August 2025.

The size. bitFlyer's audited accounts for 2025 give 272,751 ETH held, its own and its customers', at 31 December 2025. The validators then held 245,666 ETH and the multisig 2,792 ETH, about 90% of the total; a larger fleet could not have been bitFlyer's. bitFlyer also publishes the rate it earned each month; the fleet's rates are close but not distinctive enough to count either way.

Limits. Nothing bitFlyer signed or sent ties it to the multisig. bitFlyer's FAQ says it uses a staking service provider, unnamed, which fits the providers found. Explorer labels on the funding wallet are recorded as leads. The decision would change with an address list or statement from bitFlyer that excludes this multisig, or evidence that the multisig or the wallet funding it belongs to someone else.

What would change it

An address list or statement from bitFlyer that excludes this multisig, or evidence that the multisig or the wallet funding it belongs to someone else.
