Explanation

The contract 0x8659…aa83, a factory that creates per-client staking contracts, is taken to be Kiln's: validators first deposited through it are attributed to Kiln as allocator and operator, while each client keeps its own withdrawal address. 1,214 validators were deposited through it between November 2025 and October 2026, from 1,210 sending accounts, each with its own withdrawal address; about 1,060 were active in October 2026 with some 0.11M ETH, many of them compounding.

Identification. No published source names this contract. It was created by the same deployment account that created Kiln's documented version 2 batch deposit contract (decision 0034) and the contract behind MetaMask's validator staking. That is the only link, which makes this the weakest of the Kiln attributions.

Why Kiln is the allocator. A client depositing through a provider's contract has chosen that provider, who generates the keys and runs the validators; the client keeps the withdrawal address. This is the rule applied to all providers' depositor contracts (decision 0005). However, the MetaMask contract created by the same deployment account turned out to be administered by Consensys rather than Kiln (decision 0135), so a contract's creator does not by itself settle who runs it.

An explorer label on the deployment account is recorded as a lead. The decision would change with a Kiln statement contradicting it, or evidence that some depositors use the contract with keys Kiln does not run.

What would change it
