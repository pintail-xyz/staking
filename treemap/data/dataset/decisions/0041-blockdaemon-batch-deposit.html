Explanation

The contract 0x3f12…242f is a deployment of Blockdaemon's batch deposit contract: validators first deposited through it are attributed to Blockdaemon as allocator and operator, while each client keeps its own withdrawal address. 53,652 validators were deposited through it between November 2022 and October 2026, from 105 sending accounts; about 32,700 were active in October 2026 with some 1.09M ETH.

Identification. The contract's verified source is Blockdaemon's open-source batch deposit contract, differing only in minor keywords and a constructor check. It includes a distinctive feature: each batch carries a deadline, and the contract rejects a transaction submitted after it. That matches Blockdaemon's staking API, which reserves validators for a client for a fixed period and returns an unsigned transaction addressed to its batch deposit contract.

Limits. No published source names this particular deployment, and the code is open source, so another party could have deployed it. It was created through a Safe by an account with no known name, and its deposit function has no caller check. The identification rests on the code match and on how the contract is used.

Why Blockdaemon is the allocator. A client depositing through a provider's contract has chosen that provider, who runs the validators; the client keeps the withdrawal address (the rule applied to providers' depositor contracts, as for P2P.org in decision 0005). Among the clients is Celsius, whose 6,294 validators deposited through this contract in 2023 are therefore Blockdaemon's rather than Celsius's own (decision 0028).

The decision would change with a Blockdaemon statement that the contract is not its own, or evidence that another party deploys and uses it.

What would change it

A Blockdaemon statement that it is not its contract, or evidence someone else deploys it.

Evidence

Transactions and contract calls anyone can repeat on their own node.
