Explanation

Grayscale's two ether funds, the Grayscale Ethereum Trust (ETHE) and the Ethereum Mini Trust, are the principal of the validators withdrawing to these 363 addresses: the ETH is the funds', held in custody by Coinbase. In October 2026 the addresses held about 916 active validators with some 1.0M ETH.

The match with Grayscale's figures. The funds' pages (ETHE, ETH) report the amount staked: on 14 October 2025, 694,400 ETH for ETHE and 467,200 ETH for the Mini Trust, 217 and 146 lots of 3,200 ETH. Between 6 and 8 October 2025 exactly 363 deposits of 3,200 ETH were sent through Coinbase's batch deposit contract, one by each of these addresses to validators withdrawing to the same address. Each deposit was split across two or three operators in a fixed pattern (decision 0136 describes the patterns). The addresses later deposited 57 more validators in the same way. Which address belongs to which fund is not known.

Who chose the operators. Figment announced that Grayscale selected it as a staking provider for the funds, and in a later release with Figment Coinbase said that Coinbase Prime lets institutions choose their providers while it keeps custody. Coinbase is therefore the custodian of this stake, not its allocator. Each provider Grayscale hired allocates its share: Figment (decision 0136), Galaxy (identified by its graffiti), and a third operator whose validators never sign graffiti and which has not been identified.

For that third share, where no provider is identified, Grayscale itself is taken as the allocator. The decision would change if Grayscale's reported staked ether diverged from these deposits, or with evidence that the addresses hold another client's ether.

What would change it

Grayscale's staked ether diverging from these deposits, or evidence that the addresses hold another client's ether.
