Explanation

Validators withdrawing to these 1,433 addresses were the stake of Coinbase Prime custody clients, run by Coinbase on its own validator fleet, as in decision 0134; Coinbase is the allocator. The decision extends 0134 to stake it left out because 0134 considered only validators still active. The validators, 45,671 in all, were deposited through Coinbase's batch deposit contract from May 2022; they held as much as 815k ETH in January 2024, and nearly all have since exited.

Selection. The starting point was validators deposited through Coinbase's two batch deposit contracts and attributed to no one. Since the contracts are open to anyone, the route alone is no evidence.

Operator. Until early 2025 most clients wrote a default version string into block graffiti (for example "Lighthouse/v4.4.1-2841f60"); which versions a fleet runs, and when, is characteristic of the fleet. Coinbase's retail validators (decisions 0016, 0017 and 0020) provide the reference: the strings they signed in each week. Between May 2022 and February 2025 the weekly mix of non-blank graffiti on these validators matches Coinbase's retail fleet with a median similarity of 0.99. Validators of other operators score far lower on the same measure: Binance 0.38, Lido 0.22, ether.fi 0.05, Kiln 0.01. For 947 of the addresses, at least 95% of their non-blank blocks carry a string that Coinbase's retail validators signed in the same week, and none ever names an operator. Another 486 addresses never signed graffiti but pay fee recipients that only those addresses, or 0134's, also pay; recipients shared with builders or unrelated validators are not counted.

Custody. Every one of the 1,433 addresses has a depositing account that Coinbase's custody gas account 0xa863…6c0f paid gas to, or that one of Coinbase's custody wallets first funded (the tests of decision 0227).

Why Coinbase is the allocator. As in 0134, the validators ran on Coinbase's fleet and nothing names another operator. Coinbase reports running validators for its institutional clients, excluding stake it routes to partner providers. Some of this stake may have been Coinbase's non-custodial dedicated staking rather than Prime custody (usage); Coinbase would be the allocator either way.

Limits. No published source names these addresses. The operator test rests on default client graffiti, which another fleet running the same versions in the same weeks would also match; Consensys's validators do (0.98). The decision would change with an operator's name in these validators' graffiti, a version mix diverging from Coinbase's fleet, or Coinbase saying the stake was run by partners.

What would change it

A named operator in these validators' graffiti, a build mix diverging from Coinbase's fleet, or Coinbase saying this stake was run by partners.
