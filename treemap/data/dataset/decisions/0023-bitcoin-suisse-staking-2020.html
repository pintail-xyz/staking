Explanation

These seven withdrawal addresses hold Bitcoin Suisse's staking from 2020. Their validators, 13,857 deposited between November 2020 and May 2023, were the largest single group at the beacon chain's launch, 13.7% of all stake on 1 December 2020. About 8,900 were active in October 2026 with some 0.29M ETH. Bitcoin Suisse is the allocator; the stake is its clients' and possibly its own.

The graffiti. Every block proposed by a validator that has ever withdrawn to one of these addresses carries graffiti beginning "bitcoinsuisse.com", alone or followed by client codes: 140,776 blocks by 13,826 validators, with no other graffiti at all.

The deposits and keys. Every validator was deposited by one of six accounts that made no other deposits, and no other account has deposited to these addresses. All used BLS credentials, and between May and July 2023 each was changed to one of these seven addresses. Only the holder of a validator's BLS key can make that change.

The link to Bitcoin Suisse's later staking. The addresses send their withdrawals to 0x7b45…fcb1, which also receives those of Bitcoin Suisse's later staking (decision 0024). In one block on 20 October 2023 five of these addresses and two of 0024's swept to it together, so one operator runs both.

Limits. Graffiti is set by whoever runs a validator, and anyone could type a name. A fleet of this size signing Bitcoin Suisse's own domain on every block for six years is taken to be Bitcoin Suisse's, or run for it under its name. That also makes Bitcoin Suisse the likely operator, though no operator is recorded. Explorer labels on the deposit accounts are recorded as leads. The decision would change with a Bitcoin Suisse statement contradicting it, or evidence that clients chose the operators or held the withdrawal keys.

What would change it

A Bitcoin Suisse statement contradicting it, or evidence clients chose the operators or held the withdrawal keys.
