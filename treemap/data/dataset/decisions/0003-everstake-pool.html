Explanation

The contract 0xd523…be34 is the Pool of Everstake's pooled ETH staking, the pool through which Trezor's wallet stakes. Validators it deposits are attributed to Everstake as allocator. All 20,677 deposits sent through it use a single withdrawal address, 0x6be4…1e67; about 16,100 validators were active in October 2026 with some 0.51M ETH.

Trezor's use of the pool. Trezor's firmware repository gives this address as the mainnet pool for its Ethereum staking (issue 3517), and Trezor's staking guide says that staking is provided by Everstake through the Everstake staking pool. ChainSecurity's audit of Everstake's staking contracts describes a pool that deposits with its rewards treasury as withdrawal address, which is how this contract behaves.

The link from Everstake itself. Everstake's blog announces the audit of its own batch deposit contract and links the auditor's report, which gives that contract's mainnet address. On chain, the account that created that batch deposit contract also created this Pool and its withdrawal address. The account that deployed the Pool therefore also deploys a contract Everstake publicly calls its own. The Pool and its withdrawal address also share one proxy administrator.

5,465 accounts have sent deposits to the pool; as pool participants, their identities say nothing about who controls the validators. The 95 validators deposited through Everstake's batch deposit contracts are not attributed by this decision. A deployment key could in principle deploy contracts for others, so the link from Everstake remains an inference. The decision would change with an Everstake publication listing different contracts, or evidence that the deployer or the Pool is operated for another organisation.

What would change it

An Everstake publication listing different contracts, or evidence the deployer works for another organisation.

Evidence

Transactions and contract calls anyone can repeat on their own node.
