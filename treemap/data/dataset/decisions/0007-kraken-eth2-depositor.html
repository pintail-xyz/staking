Explanation

The account 0xa40d…8703 makes the beacon deposits for Kraken's ETH staking. Validators withdrawing at any time to an address its deposits used are attributed to Kraken's staking as allocator, and those addresses are taken to be held by Kraken. 105,102 validators have withdrawn to them; about 7,000 were active in October 2026 with some 1.04M ETH, most of it in compounding validators.

The deposit pattern. Between December 2020 and April 2025 the account sent 89,954 deposits. 39,111 used a single BLS withdrawal credential, and all of those changed to the address 0x210b…2a1b in April 2023, immediately after withdrawals were enabled: one holder of that key. The rest name six execution addresses, 0x210b…2a1b holding the most.

The link to Kraken. Kraken's EigenLayer restaking account is identified in decision 0010. On 21 March 2025, within 40 minutes (blocks 22,095,102 to 22,095,288), 0x210b…2a1b gave the first gas to that account and to two other deposit accounts (decisions 0008 and 0009). One account paying first gas to all three in the same window is taken to hold them all. In April and May 2023, about 591k ETH of this stake exited, which matches reports that Kraken withdrew about 556k ETH of staked Ether after its settlement with the SEC.

Following the stake as it moves. In 2026 Kraken moved its validators by consolidation requests (EIP-7251), which only a source validator's withdrawal address can send: in March into compounding validators at the same addresses, and in June into validators at eight new addresses, now holding about 850k ETH. Addresses reached in this way are followed as Kraken's, so the stake remains attributed after it moves.

Kraken's own infrastructure. In August 2025 Kraken said its ETH staking runs entirely on SSV Network's distributed validators. Every validator attributed here, and to Kraken's other deposit accounts, is registered on SSV on single-owner clusters run by accounts whose first ETH came from the gas funder of Staked, the staking provider Kraken owns. This corroborates the attribution; it names no addresses.

Limits. Kraken publishes proof of reserves but no staking addresses. The identification is as strong as decision 0010, which rests on Kraken's announced restaking and on its EigenLayer operator's self-published metadata, and which is itself weak because anyone can delegate to an operator. Six other accounts deposit through the same contract into other addresses; they may be Kraken's institutional clients and are not included here. Explorer labels are recorded as leads. The decision would change with a Kraken statement contradicting it, or evidence that these addresses hold stake for clients rather than for Kraken's staking product.

What would change it

A Kraken statement contradicting it, or evidence the addresses hold stake for clients rather than Kraken's staking.
