Explanation

The contract 0xe823…4572 is taken to be Bedrock's direct-staking contract: validators first deposited through it are attributed to Bedrock as allocator and operator, while each client keeps its own withdrawal address. It deposited 1,164 validators between March 2023 and April 2026 for fifteen clients, to twenty withdrawal addresses; they held as much as 33k ETH in March 2024, and 256 were active in October 2026, all previously unattributed.

Identification. No published source names this contract. It was created by the same account that created Bedrock's uniETH staking contract, which Bedrock's repository names (decision 0203). The same account created the reward-pool contract to which most of these validators' blocks pay their fees. The validators' graffiti follows the same client-release timeline as the uniETH validators, and a few blocks of both groups paid an address that Bedrock's repository gives for its testnet staking contract, a configuration shared with Bedrock's own setup.

Why Bedrock is the allocator. A client depositing through a provider's contract has chosen that provider, who runs the validators; the client keeps the withdrawal address (decision 0005). This is weaker than decision 0203, since no document names the contract. The decision would change with a Bedrock statement that the contract is not its own, or evidence that its validators are run by another operator.

What would change it

A Bedrock statement that the contract is not its own, or evidence that its validators are run by another operator.

Evidence

Transactions and contract calls anyone can repeat on their own node.
