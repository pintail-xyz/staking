Explanation

These 96 withdrawal addresses held Coinbase's retail staking from 2021. The validators, 38,494 in all with about 1.23M ETH, made up as much as 15.5% of all stake in mid-2021; all had exited by 2025. Coinbase is the allocator; the stake was its customers'. Coinbase ran validators both itself and through providers, and which ran these is not established, so no operator is recorded.

Deposits. Each validator was deposited directly to the deposit contract from a different single-use account, between April 2021 (when Coinbase launched ETH staking) and April 2023. In a sample of 400 deposits, each sender received exactly 32 ETH plus gas in a single transfer seven to nine blocks before depositing, and 392 of the 400 transfers came from the same small set of Coinbase hot wallets. 38,303 validators used one of 193 BLS withdrawal credentials.

The move to execution addresses. On 13 and 14 April 2023, within two days of withdrawals being enabled, all 38,303 validators with BLS credentials changed to execution credentials, into exactly 96 addresses holding 400 or 401 validators each. Making that change requires each BLS key, so a single holder of all 193 keys acted at once, in fixed-size buckets.

Where the ETH went. Every one of the 96 addresses' outgoing transactions was checked in full. All of their 1,378,914 ETH went to two accounts: rewards to 0x69e2…325e and exited stake to 0xc7bf…a019. Those two pass on most of what they receive to 0xa9d1…3e43, which receives all newly minted cbETH (the token Coinbase's own published code names as its staked ETH), or to the wallets that funded the deposits. The same operation's other deposit group is attributed in decision 0017.

There is no deposit account to anchor on, since every validator came from a fresh account, so the attribution is made for the addresses as a whole. Explorer labels on the funding wallets and the receiving accounts are recorded as leads. The decision would change with a Coinbase statement contradicting it, or evidence that the addresses belong to someone who merely funded deposits from Coinbase.

What would change it

A Coinbase statement contradicting it, or evidence the addresses belong to someone who only funded deposits from Coinbase.
