Explanation

Ebunker is the allocator of these 1,096 validators, which its own accounts registered on the SSV network, a distributed validator network, on clusters of operators Ebunker registered under its name. 250 were active in October 2026, with about 8,300 ETH; nearly all had previously been unattributed.

Ebunker's operators. Ebunker registered SSV operators 5, 55 and 67 to 70 under its name (operator 5), from the account 0x7d4f…9609, and operators 143 to 146 from another account under the name "Ebunker EtherFi". Every one of these validators runs on a cluster that includes Ebunker's operators. The other operators in some clusters, belonging to P2P.org, XHash and others, are co-operators that Ebunker's accounts chose.

Ebunker's accounts. The validators were registered on SSV by two accounts. The account behind Ebunker's operators first funded the first of them, which in turn first funded the second. Both set the same fee recipient on SSV, so their validators' block fees go to Ebunker, and some of the blocks are signed ebunker.io.

Why Ebunker is the allocator. Ebunker describes itself as a non-custodial staking pool: the withdrawal addresses are its clients', and Ebunker is the service to which the stake was handed. No operator is recorded, since each validator is run by a cluster of operators. 218 validators that Ebunker registered as a node operator for Puffer are left to Puffer (decision 0202), the service those stakers chose; HTX is recorded as principal of 380 of these validators (decision 0201). The decision would change with evidence that Ebunker's accounts register validators on SSV for a party that chose its own operators.

What would change it

Evidence that 0x0881 or 0x5e4f registers validators on SSV for a party that chose its own operators.
