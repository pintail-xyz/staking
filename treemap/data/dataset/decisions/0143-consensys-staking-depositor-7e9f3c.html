Explanation

The account 0x7e9f…6e46 is the deposit account of a Consensys Staking client. Validators whose first deposit it sent are attributed to Consensys as allocator and operator, while the client keeps its own withdrawal address. It sent the first deposits of 625 validators between 22 December 2020 and 27 December 2020, through the batch deposit contract 0x39dc…da29. All have since exited.

The route. Consensys's staking guide sends clients' batch deposits through this contract, up to 100 validators (3,200 ETH) a transaction, and lets the client choose the fee recipient. The contract was written and deployed by Staked (its repository), whose own deposit accounts use it too; they batch up to 185 validators at once and pay Staked's fee recipient. This account never sent more than 100 validators in one transaction. Their blocks paid 0xed33…85bf, a fee recipient of the client's own.

The operator. The validators' graffiti carries client version strings only. Across the client releases from early 2024, these and Consensys's other validators first used each version within hours to a day or two of one another, while the network as a whole had first used it days or weeks earlier. Coinbase's validator fleet adopts releases at almost the same times, so the timing alone does not separate the two; the route does, since this is the contract Consensys's guide names, not Coinbase's.

Why Consensys is the allocator. A client staking through a provider's platform has chosen that provider, who generates the keys and runs the validators; the client keeps the withdrawal address (the rule applied to providers' depositor contracts, decision 0005). Consensys's validator staking for MetaMask is recorded in decision 0135. Explorer labels naming the account as one of Staked's depositors are recorded as leads; they had earlier led to these validators being attributed to Staked. The decision would change with a Consensys or Staked statement contradicting it, or evidence that another operator ran these validators.

What would change it

A Consensys or Staked statement contradicting it, or evidence that another operator ran these validators.
