Dataset › Decisions › 0338-coinbase-runs-its-staking-services

Coinbase runs the validators of its retail staking, of the Prime custody stake run on its own fleet, and of those paying its own fee recipient, as it reports.

Operator or ownerService run by Coinbase from 2022-09-15, for Coinbase staking, Coinbase Prime (custody clients), Coinbase-run validators (by Coinbase fee recipient) · decided 2026-11-18

Validators that Coinbase's retail staking, its Prime custody staking run by Coinbase, or its fee recipient attribute to Coinbase are run by Coinbase, from the Merge (15 September 2022).

Grounds

  • First partyCoinbase reports 4.5M ETH on "Coinbase validators" (Q1 2026), excluding stake routed to partner providers
  • On chainCoinbase's retail validators run as one fleet: 97% of their stake runs only client builds the rest of the fleet runs (decision 0134)
  • On chainThe Prime custody validators in this service run the retail fleet's client builds week by week (decisions 0134, 0226, 0242 and 0243)
  • On chainCoinbase's retail validators pay their fees to Coinbase's own fee recipient, which passes them to Coinbase's wallets (decision 0104)

Explanation

Coinbase runs the validators of three of its staking services: its retail staking (customers' ETH it stakes for them), the stake of Coinbase Prime custody clients that runs on Coinbase's own fleet, and otherwise unattributed validators that pay Coinbase's own fee recipient. Every validator the treemap places in one of these services is recorded as run by Coinbase, unless other evidence (an operator's name in its graffiti, or a decision about that validator) names a different operator.

Coinbase's own account. Coinbase's validator performance report for Q1 2026 gives the stake "staked to Coinbase validators" as 4.5M ETH on average over the quarter, and says that it covers Coinbase's validators only, not the stake of customers who choose Coinbase's partner staking providers. In November 2026 these three services held about 3.9M ETH between them.

Which services that covers. The services were each defined on the chain's evidence that Coinbase runs them. Coinbase's retail validators form one fleet: 97% of their stake runs only client builds that the rest of the fleet runs (decision 0134). The Prime custody stake in coinbase/prime was admitted only where its validators run the retail fleet's builds week by week, or pay the same fee recipients as validators that do (decisions 0134, 0226, 0242 and 0243). Stake Coinbase's custody clients have placed with partners is not in it: Figment's (decisions 0136, 0137 and 0166), and the custody stake whose provider is not identified (coinbase/custody), which this decision does not cover. Coinbase's fee recipient receives the fees of Coinbase's retail validators and passes them to Coinbase's wallets (decision 0104). Coinbase Cloud's pooled staking (coinbase/cloud) is not covered either.

From when. From the Merge on 15 September 2022, the first date from which fee recipients and client builds in graffiti show who runs these validators. Before it, the stake is still Coinbase's to allocate, but no operator is recorded.

What would change it

Coinbase naming another company as running these validators, or a part of this stake running a fleet distinct from Coinbase's own.
