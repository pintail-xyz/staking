Explanation

The contract 0x00a0…3bb9 is one of Kiln's vPools, a pooled-staking contract that takes ETH from an integrator's users and deposits validators itself from keys registered with its own factory. Validators it deposits are attributed to Kiln as allocator and operator. It deposited 16,853 validators between August 2023 and October 2026, all withdrawing to a single withdrawal recipient contract; about 9,700 were active in October 2026 with some 0.31M ETH.

Identification. No published source names this address; the identification rests on what the contract records about itself and on its validators' graffiti. The pool's name() returns "Kiln vPool Shares", and when its factory was created it recorded "Kiln" as its operator's name. Its code, and that of the factory system it belongs to, is Kiln's vPool suite, with Kiln named as author in the verified source. Every one of the 32,205 blocks its validators have proposed is signed "Kiln.fi".

Why the code alone is not enough. Another vPool built from the same Kiln code and the same factory system names itself "Coinbase Cloud vPool Shares" and records Coinbase Cloud as its operator, and none of its blocks name Kiln (decision 0040). Running Kiln's code therefore does not by itself make a vPool Kiln's; here the pool's own records and its graffiti both name Kiln.

Why Kiln is the allocator. Users and integrators that stake through Kiln's vPool have chosen Kiln, which supplies the keys and runs the validators; this is the rule applied to providers' depositor contracts (decision 0005). The decision would change with evidence that the vPool's keys come from an operator other than Kiln.

What would change it

Evidence the vPool's keys come from an operator other than Kiln.
