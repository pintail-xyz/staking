Explanation

Validators at five more withdrawal addresses, three accounts and two EigenLayer pods that OKX's signed proof of reserves lists as its staked ETH, are OKX's staking, with OKX as allocator. In October 2026 they held about 330 active validators with some 31k ETH, all of them previously unattributed. They add to the stake of OKX's main deposit account (decision 0025).

OKX's own proof of reserves. OKX publishes a wallet file with each proof of reserves; the snapshot of 3 October 2026 was used. Among its native ETH staking rows are validators withdrawing to the three accounts, each row signed over the message "I am an OKX address", with a signature that recovers to the withdrawal address the row names: whoever made the file holds those keys. Among its EigenLayer staking rows are validators listed under two owner accounts, signed by those owners. On chain, those validators withdraw to two EigenLayer pods whose owners are those two accounts, and the file also lists both pods as OKX addresses.

What this establishes. A signed row shows that OKX holds the withdrawal key and counts the validator in its reserves as staked customer ETH, which makes OKX the allocator. Restaking through EigenLayer delegates the pods' stake to an operator without changing who chose to stake. Every validator now withdrawing to these addresses is taken to be OKX's, including any deposited after the snapshot. The file does not say who operates the validators, so no operator is recorded.

The decision would change if OKX dropped these validators from its proof of reserves while they stayed active, or with a statement that they are a client's.

What would change it

OKX dropping these validators from its proof of reserves while they stay active, or a statement that they are a client's.
