Explanation

Validators withdrawing to these 706 addresses are the stake of Coinbase Prime custody clients, run by Coinbase on its own validator fleet; Coinbase is the allocator. The exception is the validators Figment runs, attributed to Figment in decisions 0136, 0137 and 0166. In October 2026 the addresses held about 18,900 active validators with some 0.80M ETH.

Selection. The starting point was active validators first deposited through Coinbase's two batch deposit contracts. Those contracts are open to anyone, so the route alone is no evidence: an earlier attribution resting on it was reverted (decisions 0018 and 0019). Validators signing another operator's name, and those running a fleet shared with BitMine's wallets, were left out.

Custody. In most cases the account that sent the deposits received a small amount of gas from 0xa863…6c0f shortly before depositing. That account paid for 361 of the 363 deposit transactions of Grayscale's ether funds, whose custodian is Coinbase Custody (decision 0136), so an account it pays gas for is one whose keys Coinbase holds. Since January 2025, when its payments begin, 82% of the stake considered here was deposited by accounts it paid. Among earlier depositors, 66 addresses sweep their rewards together, in the same few blocks, to a wallet linked by its funding to Coinbase. Most of the remaining addresses share fee recipients with these. Custody is recorded separately in decision 0167.

Operator. Clients write their version into block graffiti as a short build string. Of the 10,905 blocks these validators proposed with a build string since early 2025, 10,904 carry a build that Coinbase's own retail validators were running at the time, and none names an operator. Week by week the mix of builds matches Coinbase's retail fleet (median similarity 0.97). For comparison, Binance's validators score 0.07 and ether.fi's 0.01; MetaMask's validator staking shows the same mix (decision 0135). Coinbase's retail fleet is itself one fleet: 97% of its stake runs only builds the rest of it runs. A fleet of that size is Coinbase's own, not one of the partners to which Coinbase routes some stake.

Why Coinbase is the allocator. Coinbase holds the accounts as custodian and runs the validators, so it chose the operator: itself. Coinbase reports running validators holding about 4.5M ETH in the first quarter of 2026, excluding stake routed to partner providers. Some of these addresses may use Coinbase's non-custodial dedicated staking service, in which Coinbase creates and runs the validators while the customer's own address receives withdrawals (usage); Coinbase would be the allocator in that case too.

In October 2026, 59 addresses were removed, 57 of them Grayscale's custody addresses (decision 0165). Their validators form a separate fleet that is not shown to be Coinbase's: they changed relays on a date when no Coinbase fleet did, and never emit build strings. Explorer labels on the accounts involved are recorded as leads. The decision would change with an operator's name in these validators' graffiti, a build mix diverging from Coinbase's fleet, or Coinbase saying that this stake is run by partners.

What would change it

A named operator in these validators' graffiti, a build mix diverging from Coinbase's fleet, or Coinbase saying this stake is run by partners.
