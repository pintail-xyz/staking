Explanation

These 179 validators, the stake of Coinbase Prime custody clients, carry the fingerprint of Figment's validator fleet and are attributed to Figment as allocator. For them this takes precedence over the decisions covering the custody addresses they withdraw to (decisions 0134 and 0167). In October 2026 they were active with about 100k ETH.

The deposits. Each was deposited through Coinbase's batch deposit contract by the custody address it withdraws to, as Coinbase Prime custody clients' validators are.

Figment's fingerprint. In one week of January 2026 these validators signed the same bare client-version graffiti string as Figment's own validators, starting and stopping in the same week: a fleet-wide configuration change, also seen on Grayscale's Figment validators (decision 0136). Independently, they voted against raising the gas limit to 60 million as Figment's validators did, and, like Figment's, never used the Aestus or Agnostic relays.

Why Figment is the allocator. Coinbase Prime clients choose their staking providers, and these validators are run by Figment's fleet; Figment is therefore the provider the clients chose. Only validators that proposed a block in that week carry the fingerprint, so other Figment-run custody validators may remain under the custody decisions. Other validators with the same fingerprint but nothing showing whose stake they are have not been attributed. The decision would change with the custody clients or Coinbase naming another operator for these validators, or their behaviour diverging from Figment's fleet.

What would change it

The custody clients or Coinbase naming another operator for these validators, or their behaviour diverging from Figment's fleet.

  • " GE117eLH120c" (client versions only, no name) in blocks between slots 14,610,294 and 14,661,589 — Figment's own validators (1,312 of them) and the Lido validators Figment operates (236) signed exactly this string, starting and stopping in the same week; outside that week they propose blank blocks.
  • blocks 23,500,000 to 23,890,000 (October and November 2025) — Like Figment's own validators, they voted to keep the 45M gas limit until the network moved to 60M; most of Coinbase's validators voted for 60M throughout.
  • MEV-Boost payloads delivered from October 2025 (ethPandaOps Xatu relay data) — None of their blocks, nor any of Figment's own, was delivered by the Aestus or Agnostic relays, which Coinbase's validators used for about a fifth of their blocks.
