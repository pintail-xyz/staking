Explanation

Validators withdrawing to 0x93db…bc8b are Crypto.com's ETH staking. The wallet deposited 5,619 validators between March 2023 and October 2026, all crediting itself; about 2,230 were active in October 2026 with some 78k ETH. Most were deposited through providers' contracts (Figment, Kiln and P2P.org), and those providers are their allocators; this decision covers the rest, chiefly the compounding validators it has deposited directly since 2026. Decision 0184 records Crypto.com as principal of all of them.

The anchor: Crypto.com's CDCETH token. Crypto.com's bug bounty programme lists 0xfe18…c38e as its CDCETH smart contract, and Crypto.com publishes the audit of the token's code; a proposal co-authored by Crypto.com to list CDCETH on Aave gives the same contract. On chain, every privileged role of the token is held by one key, which minted its whole Ethereum supply, and a separate oracle account has made all its exchange-rate updates.

The link to the staking wallet. A high-volume exchange wallet, 0xcffa…0703, sent the token's admin key and its oracle the only ETH either has ever received; Crypto.com's keys are therefore funded from it. The same wallet gave the staking wallet its first ETH, and sent it 149,383 ETH in all, nearly all it holds; the staking wallet has returned 77,203 ETH to it. The staking wallet is also its validators' own fee recipient.

Limits. The link from the token to the stake is the shared funding wallet, not a role the staking wallet exercises on the token. Other accounts that the same wallet funded look like customers' withdrawals and are not attributed. The decision would change with evidence that 0xcffa…0703 is not Crypto.com's, or that the staking wallet stakes for a single client rather than for Crypto.com's staking.

What would change it

Evidence that 0xcffa is not Crypto.com's, or that 0x93db stakes for a single client rather than for Crypto.com's staking.
