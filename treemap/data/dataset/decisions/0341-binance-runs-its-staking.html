Dataset › Decisions › 0341-binance-runs-its-staking

Binance runs the validators of its ETH staking itself, as its staking FAQ says, and they run as one fleet.

Operator or ownerService run by Binance from 2021-10-15, for Binance staking · decided 2026-11-19

Validators that Binance's staking allocates are run by Binance, from its deposit account's first deposit (15 October 2021).

Grounds

  • First partyBinance's FAQ says it handles all validator operations of its ETH Staking, setting up the validator nodes
  • On chain93% of Binance's blocks with build-only graffiti ran the build most of the fleet ran that week
  • On chainIts SSV clusters were run by their own owners, the first funded by a wallet in Binance's reserve list
  • On chainNo block of Binance's validators names another operator in its graffiti

Explanation

Binance runs the validators of its ETH staking. Every validator the treemap places in Binance's staking (decisions 0006 and 0323: those withdrawing to 0x8e60…8224, about 73,600 active validators with 3.52M ETH in November 2026) is recorded as run by Binance, unless other evidence names a different operator.

What Binance says. Binance's ETH Staking FAQ says that Binance handles all validator operations of its ETH Staking and bears the on-chain penalty risks, and that its 10% fee on staking rewards covers operating costs such as the hardware and network maintenance of setting up validator nodes. The text is taken from the Internet Archive's copy of 29 January 2025; the copy of November 2024 says the same, and later copies hold no text.

What the chain shows. The validators run as one fleet, with a single withdrawal address. In the blocks recorded here (from May 2023), their graffiti never names an operator. Until September 2024 it is Lighthouse's default version string, one version at a time in sequence; from then on it carries only the execution and consensus client versions. Over the 128 weeks with such graffiti, 93% of Binance's blocks ran the build that most of the fleet ran that week; the three weeks below 60% are changeovers from one build to the next. From November 2025 to June 2026 about 26,400 of the validators also ran on SSV, a network on which a validator's key is split among several node operators. Each of these clusters was registered by an account that also owned all four of its operators, so no outside operator held a share. The first of the three accounts was funded by 0x21a3…5549, a wallet in Binance's proof-of-reserves list of 1 October 2026, and it funded the other two. All the validators had left SSV by 21 June 2026. Earlier, all 32,319 validators deposited with BLS credentials changed them to 0x8e60…8224 in April 2023, which only the holder of all their keys could do (decision 0006).

From when. From the first deposit by Binance's deposit account, on 15 October 2021. The FAQ describes Binance's ETH staking as a whole, not a period of it.

What would change it

Binance naming another company as running these validators, or a part of this stake running a fleet distinct from the rest.
