Explanation

These 363 validators are the share of Grayscale's ether funds' stake that Figment runs, and are attributed to Figment as allocator. For them this takes precedence over the decisions covering the Coinbase Prime custody addresses they withdraw to (0134 and 0167). In October 2026 they held about 1.0M ETH, most in compounding validators.

Grayscale's deposits. Grayscale's two ether funds hold their ETH with Coinbase Custody, and their fund pages (ETHE, ETH) report the amount staked. On 14 October 2025 the pages gave 694,400 ETH staked for ETHE (217 lots of 3,200 ETH) and 467,200 ETH for the Mini Trust (146 lots). That matches exactly the 363 deposit transactions of 3,200 ETH sent between 6 and 8 October 2025 through Coinbase's batch deposit contract, each by the custody address its validators withdraw to. ETHE's later figure (204 lots on 2 January 2026) matches the 13 transactions whose validators fully exited in December 2025. Each transaction splits its 3,200 ETH across two or three operators in one of three fixed patterns, so the same position in each pattern belongs to the same operator.

Identifying Figment's share. Grayscale chose Figment as a staking provider for its ETH funds in October 2025. Two of the pattern positions behave as Figment's own validators do. In one week of January 2026 they signed the same bare client-version graffiti string as Figment's own validators, starting and stopping in the same week; a fleet-wide configuration change, also seen on other validators Figment runs. Independently, they voted against raising the gas limit to 60 million as Figment's validators did, and, like Figment's, never used the Aestus or Agnostic relays. A third position signs Galaxy's graffiti and is Galaxy's; the remaining one never signs and its operator is not identified.

The decision is made validator by validator because each custody address holds two or three operators' validators. Grayscale, the funds' manager, is recorded as principal in decision 0165. The decision would change if Grayscale named another provider for this share, or if these validators' behaviour diverged from Figment's fleet.

What would change it

Grayscale naming another provider for this share, or these validators' behaviour diverging from Figment's fleet.
