Explanation

The account 0x61db…8054 is the deposit account of a Consensys Staking client. Validators whose first deposit it sent are attributed to Consensys as allocator and operator, while the client keeps its own withdrawal address. It sent the first deposits of 402 validators between 19 December 2023 and 20 December 2023, through the batch deposit contract 0x39dc…da29. All have since exited.

The route. Consensys's staking guide sends clients' batch deposits through this contract, up to 100 validators (3,200 ETH) a transaction, and lets the client choose the fee recipient. The contract was written and deployed by Staked (its repository), whose own deposit accounts use it too; they batch up to 185 validators at once and pay Staked's fee recipient. This account never sent more than 100 validators in one transaction. Their blocks paid 0xf17d…358f, a fee recipient of the client's own. The same client also deposited through two other accounts recorded in their own decisions: the three were given their first gas by one account within a few blocks of one another, and their validators share a Safe multisig.

The operator. The validators' graffiti carries client version strings only. Across the 14 client releases they signed, their first block with each version came a median 0.62 days from the first by Consensys's other validators, while the network as a whole had first used the same version a median 10.7 days earlier. Coinbase's validator fleet adopts releases at almost the same times, so the timing alone does not separate the two; the route does, since this is the contract Consensys's guide names, not Coinbase's.

Why Consensys is the allocator. A client staking through a provider's platform has chosen that provider, who generates the keys and runs the validators; the client keeps the withdrawal address (the rule applied to providers' depositor contracts, decision 0005). Consensys's validator staking for MetaMask is recorded in decision 0135. Explorer labels are recorded as leads. The decision would change with a Consensys or Staked statement contradicting it, or evidence that another operator ran these validators.

What would change it

A Consensys or Staked statement contradicting it, or evidence that another operator ran these validators.
