Explanation

Validators withdrawing to 0xbcc3…e4a2 that no other provider claim covers are attributed to Figment as allocator, for one of its clients. 788 validators were deposited directly to the deposit contract between July 2023 and March 2025; 559 were active in October 2026 with about 18k ETH. The same client's 205 later validators, deposited through Figment's documented batch contract, are Figment's under decision 0031.

The shared configuration. One account sent every deposit to this address, first 788 directly and then 205 through Figment's contract, as Figment's other clients do. The validators of both groups paid the same fee recipient, which no other validator uses, and then both switched to the withdrawal address itself as fee recipient within the same five hours. A fee recipient is set by whoever runs the validator client, so one operator runs both groups; the group deposited through Figment's contract is Figment's, so the rest is too. Both groups' graffiti shows the same client version string only.

Why Figment is the allocator. When an exchange or other client stakes through a provider, the provider is the allocator, and the client holds the withdrawals. The client's identity rests only on explorer and community labels, which are recorded as leads, so it is not named here; the attribution to Figment does not depend on it.

Limits. The client could run both groups' validator clients itself and set the fee recipient, with Figment only depositing the later 205. The decision would change with evidence that another operator runs the directly deposited validators, or that the client chooses their operators itself.

What would change it

Evidence that another operator runs the directly deposited validators, or that the client chooses their operators itself.
