Explanation

Coinbase holds these 1,313 withdrawal addresses in custody for clients of Coinbase Prime, its institutional service. The decision records custody only. Who chose the staking provider for each address is settled by other decisions: where one names a provider (Coinbase's own fleet under 0134, Figment under 0136, 0137 and 0166, or a provider named in graffiti or by a fee pool), that provider is the allocator; where a principal is known (Grayscale's ether funds, 0165), the principal follows. Only where nothing else shows an allocator is Coinbase, as custodian, assumed to be it.

The custody evidence. Each address's validators were deposited by accounts that Coinbase's custody systems funded or paid gas for. The key account is 0xa863…6c0f, which pays small amounts of gas to accounts shortly before they deposit. It paid for 361 of the 363 deposit transactions of Grayscale's two ether funds, whose fund pages (ETHE, ETH) name Coinbase Custody Trust Company as custodian. An account whose gas Coinbase pays is one whose keys Coinbase holds. Of the 541 addresses carried over from decision 0114, 539 have a depositing account that 0xa863…6c0f paid gas to, and 475 of those accounts were also first funded by one of two Coinbase custody wallets. The addresses from decision 0134 rest on the evidence set out there.

Why custody is recorded apart from allocation. Coinbase Prime clients choose among staking providers; Figment's announcement with Coinbase Prime describes one such option. Custody alone does not show who runs the validators.

Wallets into which Coinbase's retail staking pays out are not treated as evidence of custody: they also fund ordinary customers' withdrawals, including home stakers. Two addresses that rested on them alone were removed. Explorer labels on the custody wallets are recorded as leads. The decision would change with evidence that an address is held by its owner, or by another custodian, rather than by Coinbase for a Prime client.
