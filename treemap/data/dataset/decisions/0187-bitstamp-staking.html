Grounds

  • Third partyBitstamp's records, filed in a U.S. court case, name 0x1522 as its ETH wallet; 0x00bd deployed and operates it
  • On chain0x538d's ETH came from 0x1522, 0x00bd and a wallet it co-signs, which also sent 0x3a1e its only ETH

Explanation

Validators withdrawing to 0x88a4…c20b or 0xf666…48ae are the staking of Bitstamp, the exchange, with Bitstamp as allocator. In October 2026 they held about 1,540 active validators with some 49k ETH. Both addresses are BitGo multisig wallets, each with three signers. Bitstamp says it delegates its customers' stake to a staking node operator. Since 30 October 2026 that operator is recorded as Staked: the validators run on SSV operators funded from the same source as those of Staked's own validators, and their early blocks paid Staked's fee recipient. Bitstamp was acquired by Robinhood in 2025; the stake is recorded under Bitstamp, the service that holds it.

The anchor. An expert report filed in a U.S. court case (CFPB v. Consumer Advocacy Center, Doc. 364-3) cites Bitstamp's records giving 0x1522…e428 as the address that sent a customer's ETH withdrawals. The account 0x00bd…74ed deployed that contract in 2017 and has called it about two million times: Bitstamp's wallet operator.

The deposit keys. The signer that deployed 0x88a4…c20b got its ETH only from Bitstamp's wallet contract, from its operator, and from a BitGo wallet it co-signs; it made every deposit to 0x88a4…c20b, from 2021 (with BLS credentials, later changed to the address) to February 2025. The signer that deployed 0xf666…48ae got its only ETH from that same co-signed wallet, in transactions sent by the first signer, and has made every deposit to 0xf666…48ae since February 2025.

Where the ETH goes. Withdrawals from 0x88a4…c20b pass through further wallets to 0x7f60…b752, a hot wallet into which Bitstamp's wallet contract empties, and the second signer operates thousands of contracts that also empty into it. The hot wallet's identification as Bitstamp's is itself inferred.

Limits. No Bitstamp document names the signers or the staking wallets. Bitstamp Europe's accounts for 2021 to 2023 list staked ether, consistent with the 2021 deposits. The decision would change with evidence that the signers act for a client of Bitstamp, or for BitGo, rather than for Bitstamp's own staking.

What would change it

Evidence that 0x538d or 0x3a1e acts for a client of Bitstamp, or for BitGo, rather than for Bitstamp's own staking.
