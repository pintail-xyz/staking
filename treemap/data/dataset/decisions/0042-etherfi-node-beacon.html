Grounds

Explanation

ether.fi's validators are found through the contract 0x3c55…c03f, the template (beacon) from which ether.fi creates a node contract for each validator or group of validators. Validators withdrawing to an ether.fi node contract, or to an EigenLayer pod that a node owns, are attributed to ether.fi's staking as allocator. They numbered about 103,000 in all, 24,540 of them active in October 2026 with about 1.7M ETH.

Identification. The beacon was created by, and is owned by, 0x25e8…912d. ether.fi's deployment records name that account as its StakingManager, and ether.fi's code states that each node contract owns one EigenLayer pod. 23,330 node contracts have been created from the beacon. 488 of them receive withdrawals directly (one validator each, from May 2023); the other 23,308 each own an EigenLayer pod that their validators withdraw to (from November 2023). The pods' owners are read from EigenLayer's pod contracts.

Why ether.fi is the allocator. Node operators obtain validators through ether.fi's auction and whitelist, and the stake comes from its liquidity pool (eETH) or, in its early period, from stakers who chose ether.fi. Most deposits were sent through two Safe multisigs; they are not needed to identify the stake, since the withdrawal side identifies ether.fi directly. No operator is recorded, because the auction records that would name one are not read. No validator here has a conflicting allocator claim.

An explorer label naming the beacon as ether.fi's is recorded as a lead. The decision would change with an ether.fi statement contradicting it, or evidence that some nodes were created for stakers who chose their own operator.

What would change it

An ether.fi statement contradicting it, or evidence some nodes serve stakers who chose their own operator.

Evidence

Transactions and contract calls anyone can repeat on their own node.
