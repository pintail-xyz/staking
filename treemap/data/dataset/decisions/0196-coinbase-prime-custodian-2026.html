Explanation

Coinbase holds these 167 withdrawal addresses in custody for Coinbase Prime clients. The decision records custody only: who chose and runs the staking is settled elsewhere, and where nothing else shows an allocator Coinbase is assumed to be it. In October 2026 the addresses held about 545 active validators with some 0.62M ETH, mostly compounding validators.

The custody test. Each address sent its own deposits through Coinbase's batch deposit contract, and in the 50 blocks before its first deposit Coinbase's custody gas account 0xa863…6c0f sent it a small amount of gas. That account paid for 361 of the 363 deposit transactions of Grayscale's ether funds, whose fund pages (ETHE, ETH) name Coinbase Custody as custodian; an account whose gas Coinbase pays is one whose keys Coinbase holds. Many of the accounts were also funded by Coinbase's custody wallets.

Why custody is recorded apart from allocation. Coinbase Prime clients choose their staking providers, as Figment's announcement with Coinbase Prime describes. 140 of these addresses also hold validators that sign Galaxy's graffiti, and Galaxy runs custody stake for Prime clients; the remaining validators propose blank blocks or carry client version strings only. Validators that graffiti or another claim gives to Galaxy or Figment keep it.

Not included: 38 accounts depositing through the same contracts without the gas payment, holding about 42k ETH. The absence of the payment proves nothing, but nothing else here shows custody. The decision would change with evidence that an address is held by its owner, or by another custodian, rather than by Coinbase for a Prime client.

What would change it

Evidence that an address is held by its owner or another custodian, not by Coinbase for a Prime client.
