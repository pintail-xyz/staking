Explanation

The account 0x7846…3c48 deposited validators that Staked runs: validators whose first deposit it sent are attributed to Staked as allocator and operator, while the client keeps its own withdrawal address. It sent the first deposits of 867 validators between 10 July 2021 and 18 March 2024. All have since exited.

The route. Staked's repository names 0x39dc…da29 as its batch deposit contract, and Staked's FAQ says it takes up to 185 validators a transaction. Staked ran validators for clients, who deposited from their own wallets and kept their withdrawal keys. The same contract is also the route Consensys documents for its staking clients, whose accounts never batch more than 100 validators and pay fee recipients of their own (decisions 0142 to 0149 and 0229 to 0236); this account's validators pay Staked's instead.

The fee recipient. After the Merge, 847 of its 847 validators that proposed blocks paid their rewards to Staked's fee recipient 0xe94f…6ca0c, in 6,597 of 6,619 blocks. That address is Staked's execution-reward pool (decision 0153): Staked says it collects its validators' execution-layer rewards and pays them on to stakers after its commission, and this address has made over 9,000 payments to stakers. Its first incoming transfer came from a wallet linked by funding to the account that created Staked's batch contract, and that wallet has received 800 ETH of commission from it.

Staked has been part of Kraken since December 2021; it is kept as its own provider. Explorer labels naming the account as one of Staked's depositors are recorded as leads. The decision would change with a Staked or Kraken statement contradicting it, or evidence that the account deposited keys Staked did not run.
