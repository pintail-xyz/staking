Explanation

The account 0x6682…6ba8, whose ENS name is whale.eth, deposited 710 validators from its own ETH between November and December 2020: its validators, and those withdrawing to the address they moved to, are attributed to whale.eth's own staking, with the account as allocator. They made up 1.8% of all stake at the beacon chain's launch, and all exited in April 2023.

Identification. The account's ENS primary name is whale.eth, and whale.eth resolves back to the account. The ENS name is used as the account's own public label; no person is named.

On chain. The account was first funded in January 2020 and has traded actively since, wrapping and swapping ETH on decentralised exchanges, as a private holder does. It deposited the 710 validators, 22,720 ETH, with BLS credentials, all later changed to a single address, which paid out the ETH after the validators exited.

Why not PieDAO. An explorer label once named the account as a depositor for PieDAO, and these validators had been attributed to PieDAO on that basis alone. PieDAO's published documentation and code mention neither the account nor its withdrawal address. The label is recorded as a lead. The decision would change with a PieDAO or Auxo statement that the account deposited for it, or evidence that the stake belonged to someone else.

What would change it
