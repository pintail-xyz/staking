Explanation

These 412 withdrawal addresses hold Coinbase's retail staking from April 2023, continuing the arrangement of its 2021 staking (decisions 0016 and 0017). Between April 2023 and September 2026 they received 164,800 validators; 88,151 were active in October 2026 with about 2.8M ETH. Coinbase is the allocator; the stake belongs to its customers. No operator is recorded.

The pattern. Each address holds exactly 400 validators, every one deposited directly to the deposit contract, with credentials naming the address, from a sender account that made no other deposit. Each sender received its 32 ETH in a single transfer about ten blocks before depositing. For 387 of the 412 addresses the funding came from the same small set of hot wallets that funded the 2021 staking; for one it came from an account those wallets first funded; for 24 it came from 0x7c87…b102, a high-volume wallet.

Where the ETH goes. Every address's outgoing transactions were checked in full. All 411 that have swept rewards sent them to 0x69e2…325e, and 387 sent their exited stake to 0xc7bf…a019. Those two accounts pass 84% and 95% of what they receive to 0xa9d1…3e43 or to the wallets that funded the deposits. 0xa9d1…3e43 receives all newly minted cbETH, the token Coinbase's own published code (its AgentKit token list) names as its staked ETH. Rewards and principal therefore return to Coinbase.

The 24 addresses funded by 0x7c87…b102 sweep their rewards to the same account as the others, but send their exited stake to 0x6dca…3287, which has returned all it received (127,552 ETH) to 0x7c87…b102. That fits a client staking through Coinbase: Coinbase runs the validators and collects their rewards, while the principal returns to its source. Coinbase remains the allocator.

Explorer labels on the funding wallets are recorded as leads, not relied on. The decision would change with a Coinbase statement contradicting it, or evidence that any of these addresses belongs to someone else, notably the 24 whose exited stake goes elsewhere.

What would change it

A Coinbase statement contradicting it, or evidence that addresses belong to someone else: notably 24 that return exited stake to the account that funded them.
