Explanation

Validators withdrawing to these 23 Safe multisigs are the staking of MaiCoin, the Taiwanese exchange operator, with MaiCoin as allocator. In October 2026 they held 843 active validators with about 27k ETH, all previously unattributed. MaiCoin offers its customers ETH staking; who runs the validators is not established.

The anchor. A tracing report filed in a U.S. court case (2:24-cv-06769, exhibit 1-1) names 0xf803…6975 as a MaiCoin deposit address in March 2023. That address was given gas by, and swept everything it received to, 0x477b…6b6f, a hot wallet.

The deposits. The hot wallet funded the deposit key 0x5a43…f212 with 27,018 ETH in 263 transfers from September 2023, and the key's first deposit followed within minutes of its first funding. That key and a second, 0x8e69…b1c4, which deposited to some of the same Safes, made every deposit to these 23 Safes, mostly in groups of 40 or 10 validators per Safe. Rewards withdrawn from the Safes flow back to the first key.

The Safes. Each is a 3-of-6 Safe with the same six owners, who also own the Safes receiving the validators' block rewards. The hot wallet also funded the creator and owner of MAX, MaiCoin's exchange token.

Limits. The court record names a customer deposit address, not the staking; the link from the hot wallet rests on funding, as for Bitstamp (decision 0187). MaiCoin's own pages describe its staking product but name no address. Explorer labels are recorded as leads. The decision would change with evidence that the hot wallet is not MaiCoin's, or that the deposit keys or the Safes' owners act for a MaiCoin client rather than MaiCoin.

What would change it

Evidence that 0x477b is not MaiCoin's, or that 0x5a43, 0x8e69 or the Safes' owners act for a MaiCoin client rather than MaiCoin.
