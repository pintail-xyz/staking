Explanation

These 93 withdrawal addresses held more of Coinbase's retail staking from 2021, by the same arrangement as decision 0016. The validators, 37,291 in all with about 1.19M ETH, made up as much as 6.8% of all stake in early 2023; all had exited by April 2025. They form a separate group from 0016's only because their deposit senders and BLS keys do not overlap. Coinbase is the allocator; the stake was its customers'. No operator is recorded.

Deposits. Between March 2021 and April 2023 the validators were deposited steadily, 36,358 of them directly from single-use accounts and 933 through Coinbase's batch deposit contract. In a sample of 200 deposits, each sender received exactly 32 ETH plus gas in a single transfer a few blocks before depositing (median ten blocks), and 198 of the transfers came from the same Coinbase hot wallets that funded 0016's deposits. 37,190 validators used one of 197 BLS withdrawal credentials.

The move to execution addresses. On 14 and 15 April 2023, two days after withdrawals were enabled, all 37,190 validators with BLS credentials changed to execution credentials, into exactly 93 addresses holding 400 or 401 validators each: one holder of all the keys acting at once, in fixed-size buckets, as in 0016.

Where the ETH went. Every address's outgoing transactions were checked in full. 92 of the 93 sent everything to the same two accounts as 0016's: rewards to 0x69e2…325e and exited stake to 0xc7bf…a019. The remaining address also sent 32 ETH, one validator's stake, to an account about which nothing is known. Those two accounts pass most of what they receive to 0xa9d1…3e43, which receives all newly minted cbETH (named as Coinbase's staked ETH token in Coinbase's own published code), or to the wallets that funded the deposits.

Explorer labels on the counterparties are recorded as leads. The decision would change with a Coinbase statement contradicting it, or evidence that the addresses belong to someone who merely funded deposits from Coinbase.

What would change it

A Coinbase statement contradicting it, or evidence the addresses belong to someone who only funded deposits from Coinbase.
